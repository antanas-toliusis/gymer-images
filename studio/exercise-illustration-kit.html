Exercise Illustration Kit

The approved Barbell Reverse Curl and the rules for drawing every other exercise in the same style.

Look and feel

  • Black athletic silhouette on white, close to classic gym pictograms but more refined.
  • Lean, athletic and balanced build: V-shaped torso, slim arms, legs slightly fuller than the upper body.
  • Thin white lines cut into the black for pecs, abs, knees and the gap between the legs.
  • A thin white halo around limbs and equipment wherever they overlap the body.
  • Two frames, start on the left and end on the right. No text inside the image.
  • The end frame shows a faded copy of the equipment at its start position, plus curved motion arrows.

Avoid: colour, gradients on the figure, a full outline around the figure, faces, text, bulky bodybuilder proportions.

Layer order

  1. Ghost equipment (end frame only)
  2. Body: shadow, head, torso, shorts, legs, feet, leg gap, muscle lines
  3. Arms: upper arm, then forearm, with halos
  4. Equipment halo, then equipment
  5. Hands, on top of the bar so the grip reads
  6. Motion arrows (end frame only)

Making a new exercise

  1. Copy the reference SVG and keep its <defs> unchanged.
  2. Decide the start and end pose. Change only arm rotations, equipment position and hand positions.
  3. For seated or bent-over exercises, add a new body pose next to #body with the same proportions and line weights.
  4. Put the ghost equipment in the end frame at the start position, with arrows from the ghost to the new position.
  5. Check the PNG at full size and at thumbnail size (about 120 px wide): arms visible, halos separating every overlap, lean build, arrows clear of plates and body.
  6. Name files in kebab-case, for example reverse-wrist-curl.svg, adding -dumbbell when both versions exist.
